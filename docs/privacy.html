Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-08-13 (added disclosures for the Currency widget and the Iframe widget)

StartGrid is a browser extension (Firefox & Chrome) that replaces your new tab page with a customizable widget grid. This policy explains what data StartGrid accesses, why, and how it's stored — in particular for the optional Google Calendar, Google Tasks, and Outlook Calendar/Mail widgets, which require signing in with your Google or Microsoft account, and the optional Obsidian widgets, which read notes from a vault on your own device. All of these are off by default and do nothing until you add the widget and connect it.

Summary

  • StartGrid does not operate a backend server that stores your personal data. Your account tokens and cached widget data are stored locally in your browser's extension storage (browser.storage.local). Your layout and widget settings are stored in your browser's built-in extension sync storage (browser.storage.sync) so they follow you across devices — this is your browser's own sync service (Google Chrome Sync or Mozilla Sync), not a StartGrid server. The only server StartGrid's developer operates is a stateless Cloudflare Worker relay used during OAuth token exchange and for API-key-protected image providers (see "Data security"), which does not log or retain anything it relays.
  • Google and Microsoft account access is entirely optional, off by default, and only activates when you click "Connect" inside the relevant widget's settings.
  • All Google and Microsoft data access is read-only. StartGrid never sends, deletes, modifies, or shares your calendar or mail data.
  • The optional Obsidian widgets talk only to your own device — either through Obsidian's obsidian:// link scheme or through a plugin you install yourself that listens on 127.0.0.1. No note content, file path, or search term is ever transmitted off your machine, to StartGrid's developer or to anyone else.
  • StartGrid does not sell, rent, or share your data with third parties or advertisers.

Data accessed via Google Sign-In

When you click "Connect" in the Calendar or To-Do widget, StartGrid opens Google's standard OAuth consent screen. If you approve, StartGrid receives an access token scoped to only the permissions listed below — nothing else in your Google Account is touched.

ScopeWhat it's used for
calendar.readonly Fetches upcoming events from your primary calendar, plus any additional Google calendars you select in the widget's "My Calendars" list — title, start/end time, location, color, and a link back to the event — via a field-restricted Calendar API request. Used to render the Calendar widget. No event is ever created, edited, or deleted.
tasks.readonly Fetches your upcoming Google Tasks — title and due date only — via a field-restricted Tasks API request. Used to render the optional To-Do widget's Google Tasks view. No task is ever created, edited, completed, or deleted; the extension cannot write to your Tasks account at all with this scope.
openid, email Used only to show which Google Account is currently connected in the widget's settings panel (e.g. "Connected as name@example.com"), so you can confirm or switch accounts.

Data accessed via Microsoft Sign-In

StartGrid also includes Outlook Calendar and Outlook Mail widgets, which use the same OAuth pattern with a Microsoft Account instead of Google. Connecting requests the following read-only Microsoft Graph scopes: Mail.Read (read-only access to your Outlook mail, used to show unread/recent messages in the Outlook Mail widget), Calendars.Read (read-only access to your Outlook calendar events, used by the Outlook Calendar widget), and offline_access, openid, email (to keep the connection alive and show which account is connected). The same storage, security, and deletion practices described in this policy for Google apply equally to Microsoft data: tokens are kept only in local browser storage (never in sync storage), fetched mail and events stay in the open tab's memory and are never written to storage, the token exchange is relayed through the same Cloudflare Worker (which attaches the Microsoft client secret server-side and does not log or retain what it relays), and disconnecting or uninstalling removes all locally stored Microsoft data immediately. Note one difference from Google: Microsoft's identity platform provides no per-application revocation endpoint, so "Disconnect account" deletes StartGrid's local copy of your tokens but cannot invalidate them on Microsoft's side. To revoke the grant itself, use your Microsoft account's app permissions page. The Outlook Mail widget reads each message's subject, sender name and address, received time, read/unread state, and the short preview snippet Microsoft returns — never full message bodies or attachments — and holds them in memory only, never writing them to storage.

Where this data is stored

Your Google access token and refresh token (if issued) are stored only in your browser's local extension storage (browser.storage.local), on your own device — never in sync storage, so they are never uploaded to your browser account or carried to another device. Fetched calendar events are held in the page's memory only and are never written to storage at all. None of this is transmitted to any server operated by StartGrid or its developer, other than the OAuth token exchange described below. Disconnecting the account (or uninstalling the extension) removes this data immediately.

Separately, your grid layout, widget settings, and — if you switch a Notes widget to "Cloud" storage — that note's text are kept in the browser's extension sync storage (browser.storage.sync). Your browser, not StartGrid, replicates that data across the devices where you are signed in, under your Google Chrome or Mozilla account and that provider's own privacy policy. If you prefer a note to stay on one device, set its storage to "Local" in the widget's settings; StartGrid then keeps that note's text in browser.storage.local, which never syncs.

Data security

StartGrid limits the security surface for your Google data by design:

  • All Google API and OAuth traffic (accounts.google.com, oauth2.googleapis.com, www.googleapis.com) is transmitted exclusively over HTTPS/TLS-encrypted connections — StartGrid never makes an unencrypted request for Google data.
  • Your access token and refresh token are kept only in browser.storage.local, which is sandboxed to the StartGrid extension by the browser itself; no other extension, website, or the extension's developer can read this storage. Your calendar events and Google Tasks are never written to storage at all — they exist only in the open tab's memory.
  • The sign-in and authorization step uses PKCE directly between your browser and Google. The token exchange is the exception: because Google's OAuth client type requires a client_secret that cannot safely live inside extension source code, that request is relayed through a Cloudflare Worker operated by StartGrid's developer (startgrid-api-proxy.startgrid.workers.dev), which attaches the secret server-side before forwarding to Google over HTTPS. This applies both to the initial exchange and to each later refresh of an expired access token, so your refresh token passes through the relay each time it is used. The Worker is stateless — it has no database or storage attached and does not log, store, or retain the code, token, or response it passes through.
  • Token revocation (when you disconnect) is sent directly from your browser to Google, not through the Worker.
  • StartGrid's settings backup/export feature deliberately excludes your account tokens: an exported backup file contains your layout and widget settings only, so a backup can never carry a working Google or Microsoft credential out of the browser's protected storage. Restoring a backup likewise ignores any token data a file might contain.
  • StartGrid has no database or backend beyond this relay function, so there is no server-side storage where your Google data is retained or could be breached.

Data retention and deletion

StartGrid stores your Google access token and refresh token in your browser's local extension storage for as long as you keep the Calendar or To-Do (Google Tasks) widget connected — both widgets share the same Google sign-in, so one token covers both. Calendar events and Google Tasks themselves are never written to storage at all: they are fetched fresh into the page's memory on each load and discarded the moment you close the tab, so no copy of your event or task data survives a browser restart. Nothing is accumulated or archived, and there is no server-side copy anywhere.

You can delete this data at any time, in any of the following ways:

  • Click "Disconnect account" in the Calendar or To-Do widget's settings panel — this sends a revocation request for the OAuth token to Google and deletes the locally stored token. Since both widgets share one Google sign-in, disconnecting from either one disconnects both. The local deletion always happens, even if the network revocation request fails; in that case the token is gone from your device and expires on Google's side shortly after.
  • Revoke StartGrid's access from your Google Account permissions page — this invalidates the token on Google's side immediately. StartGrid's stored copy stops working at once and is cleared automatically the next time it tries to refresh it (at most one hour later, or sooner if you reload the page).
  • Use "Factory Reset" in the settings panel — this erases all of StartGrid's stored data, including any Google or Microsoft tokens.
  • Uninstall the extension — your browser automatically deletes all of StartGrid's local storage, including any Google tokens and cached data, immediately and permanently.

Bookmarks, links, and site icons

The Bookmark Folder, Bookmark Search, and Quicklinks widgets read your browser's bookmark tree (bookmarks permission, requested only when you add one of these widgets) to display and navigate your bookmarks from the new tab page. Your bookmark titles, URLs, and folder structure are never sent to StartGrid's developer or to any StartGrid-operated server.

To show a site icon next to each bookmark or quicklink, StartGrid requests that site's favicon from a public favicon service. This means the domain name of each displayed bookmark or quicklink (for example github.com — never the full URL, page title, or folder name) is sent to one of the following third parties, in this order, falling through only if the previous one has no icon:

  • DuckDuckGo (icons.duckduckgo.com) — used by all three widgets
  • Google's public favicon service (www.google.com/s2/favicons) — Quicklinks fallback only. This is an unauthenticated public endpoint; it is not connected to your Google Account and no token is sent with it.
  • unavatar.io — Quicklinks final fallback

These requests are made by your browser directly to those services; StartGrid's developer never sees them. To avoid them entirely for a given quicklink, set its icon to a custom URL or an uploaded image in the widget's settings.

Clicking a bookmark or quicklink opens it in a tab. StartGrid does not request the tabs permission and cannot read your open tabs' URLs, titles, or content.

Obsidian widgets (your notes)

StartGrid offers optional widgets that read and write notes in a local Obsidian vault: Quick Capture, Daily Note, Pinned Note, Vault Search, and Random Note. None of them are active unless you add them.

Nothing about your vault ever leaves your device. There is no StartGrid server involved in these widgets at any point — no relay, no proxy, no analytics. Your note contents, note titles, file paths, folder names, tags, and search terms are never transmitted to StartGrid's developer, to the Cloudflare Worker described above, or to any third party.

There are two ways these widgets reach Obsidian, and they differ in what they need:

  • Quick Capture, by default, uses Obsidian's own obsidian:// link scheme — the same mechanism as clicking an Obsidian link anywhere else on your system. Your operating system hands the text to the Obsidian app. This requires no permission from StartGrid at all and no setup inside Obsidian.
  • The other four widgets (and Quick Capture, if you choose to connect it) talk to the Local REST API with MCP community plugin, which you install and enable inside Obsidian yourself. That plugin runs a small server on your own machine at 127.0.0.1, the loopback address. Requests to it never touch a network. This is what the optional http://127.0.0.1/* host permission is for, and StartGrid only asks for it when you click "Allow local access".

The plugin protects its server with an API key that you copy from its settings into StartGrid. That key is stored only in browser.storage.local on the device where you entered it — deliberately never in sync storage, so it is not uploaded to your Google or Mozilla account or carried to another device.

Note contents fetched from your vault mostly live in the open tab's memory only and are discarded when you close the tab, with two exceptions written to browser.storage.local on your device (never sync storage, never transmitted anywhere): Daily Note and Pinned Note each cache the last-loaded content of the specific note path you've configured, so the widget can still show something if a later refresh fails instead of going blank; and the Random Note widget caches a list of your vault's Markdown file paths (not their contents) for six hours, so it doesn't have to re-scan your vault on every new tab. The file-path cache can be cleared at any time with "Rebuild vault index" in Random Note's settings; the note-content caches are simply overwritten the next time that widget successfully reloads its note. Neither survives long after removing the corresponding widget's connection in practice, and both are wiped along with everything else if you uninstall StartGrid.

Daily Note can tick checkboxes off in today's note. Daily Note and Pinned Note can also replace a note's entire contents when you use their edit button (the pencil icon), which first re-checks that the note hasn't changed in Obsidian since it was loaded, refusing to save over a conflicting edit. Quick Capture appends the text you type. These are the only writes StartGrid makes to your vault; it never deletes notes, and it never modifies a note you have not pointed a widget at. Revoking the host permission, clicking "Disconnect", or quitting Obsidian stops all of it immediately.

Location (Weather widget and background weather effect)

The optional Weather widget, if you enable it, lets you search for a location by name; the place name you type is sent to the third-party weather provider Open-Meteo to resolve it to coordinates and fetch local weather data — StartGrid's developer never receives or stores this location data. StartGrid does not access your device's geolocation. The location you choose is saved with that widget's settings in the browser's sync storage described above, so the same location appears on your other signed-in devices; removing the Weather widget removes it.

Separately, the optional background weather effect (Settings → Settings, off by default) — which renders animated rain or snow behind the widget grid — has its own independently-set location, used only to decide whether to show rain, snow, or nothing. It is not tied to the Weather widget and works whether or not that widget is on your grid. It is set the same way, by searching for a location by name, and sends the resulting coordinates directly to Open-Meteo the same way; the resulting location is saved with your global settings in sync storage.

If you turn on the Weather widget's optional "Open detailed forecast on click" setting (off by default), clicking the widget opens a new browser tab to a forecast page at the provider you chose in its settings — Google Search, Windy, or WetterOnline. This is an ordinary outbound link, the same as clicking any other link on a web page: StartGrid does not send anything to that site itself, but your browser's normal navigation request (which includes your IP address and, depending on the provider, the city name or coordinates in the URL so the right forecast loads) goes directly to whichever site you picked, subject to that site's own privacy policy.

Rain Radar widget (map and radar tiles)

The optional Rain Radar widget, if you add it, shows a map with a live precipitation overlay for a location you choose. It is off by default and does nothing until added. Rendering it requires two third-party tile services, contacted directly by your browser:

  • CARTO (basemaps.cartocdn.com) — supplies the base map image tiles (streets, borders, place names). Requests are for map tiles by coordinate grid reference, not a raw latitude/longitude query parameter, though the tiles requested do reveal the general area being viewed, the same as loading any online map.
  • RainViewer (api.rainviewer.com, tile.rainviewer.com) — supplies the radar precipitation overlay, the same way.

Both are free public tile services that require no API key or account; StartGrid's developer never receives or stores any of these requests, and neither service is passed your Google Account, email, or any other personal data — only your IP address, as with any web request, and the map area currently in view. The city name and coordinates you configure for this widget are saved with its settings in the browser's sync storage described above; removing the widget removes them.

Other data StartGrid accesses

Optional background-image providers fetch a public photo URL and its attribution text. Each sends your IP address to the provider, as any web request does; none involves your Google Account or any personal data:

  • Unsplash — routed through StartGrid's Cloudflare Worker so the API key stays server-side. If you use search or collection mode, the search text or collection ID you type is included in the request. The photo itself is then loaded directly from Unsplash's image CDN.
  • NASA Astronomy Picture of the Day — routed through the same Worker for the same reason.
  • Bing daily wallpaper — fetched from the community-run mirror bing.npanuhin.me (not operated by Microsoft or by StartGrid), because Microsoft's own endpoint does not permit direct browser requests.
  • Wikimedia Picture of the Day — fetched directly from api.wikimedia.org.
  • Online Image / custom icon URL — if you paste an image URL yourself, your browser loads it directly from whatever host you specified.

The Weather widget's "search by name" option sends the place name you type to Open-Meteo's geocoding service to resolve it to coordinates.

The optional Currency widget, if you add it, fetches exchange rates from Frankfurter (free, no API key, ECB rate data), requested directly by your browser. Only the base and target currency codes you select are sent — no other data. Frankfurter's developer never receives your Google Account, email, or any other personal data, only your IP address as with any web request.

The optional Iframe widget, if you add it, displays a webpage of your choosing inside a widget on your dashboard. The URL you enter is loaded directly by your browser, the same as opening it in a tab — StartGrid does not proxy, inspect, or record it. That site sees an ordinary page visit (your IP address and whatever else it would normally see from any visitor), subject to that site's own privacy policy. Many sites block being embedded this way and simply refuse to load.

The optional RSS Feed widget works the same way as Unsplash/NASA above: the feed URL you enter is fetched server-side through StartGrid's Cloudflare Worker, not directly from your browser (most RSS/Atom feeds don't permit direct cross-origin browser requests). Because the Worker makes the request, the feed host sees the Worker's network location, not yours. The feed content is parsed in your browser, never on the Worker.

The Cloudflare Worker referenced above (startgrid-api-proxy.startgrid.workers.dev) exists solely to keep third-party API keys and OAuth client secrets out of the extension's source code, and — for the RSS Feed widget — to work around feeds that don't support direct browser requests. It is stateless, has no database or storage attached, and does not log, store, or inspect request contents beyond what is needed to forward the call.

Your controls

  • Each widget's Google connection can be disconnected at any time from its settings panel, which asks Google to revoke the token and deletes the stored tokens for that widget from your device.
  • You can also revoke StartGrid's access at any time from your Google Account permissions page, or for Microsoft from your Microsoft account's app permissions page.
  • "Factory Reset" in the settings panel erases everything StartGrid has stored, in both local and sync storage.
  • Uninstalling the extension removes all locally stored data.
